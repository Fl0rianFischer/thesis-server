# Universe

What the opportunities brief looks at, beyond the holdings in `watchlist.md`.
Holdings live there and are not repeated here — this file is the *rest* of the
search space.

Same principle as the watchlist: the line under each entry is what does the
filtering. A specific line produces a short, signal-dense brief; a vague one
produces a headline feed. The "Never" section at the bottom matters just as much
as the other two.

Editing this file is a commit and a deploy, like the watchlist — versioned and
revertible.

> **The "Would need" lines are drafts.** They were written from what each
> business is, not from a view you stated, and they are the highest-value thing
> in this file to edit. The agent stays quiet until a test is met, so a test that
> isn't yours produces either silence about something you cared about or noise
> about something you didn't.

## Themes

Secular shifts to have exposure to, on a horizon of years. The agent uses these
to notice when a name attached to one of them gets cheap, and to ignore names
attached to nothing here.

## The physical layer under AI
**Looking for:** the constraint that outlasts the model race — power delivery,
cooling, optical interconnect, memory bandwidth, fabrication. Companies whose
revenue is contracted or capacity-limited rather than driven by which lab is
ahead this quarter. Not the model developers themselves.

## Software sold by the seat, as AI changes what a seat is
**Looking for:** systems of record with real switching costs that are priced per
user or per employee. The question on all of them is the same — whether AI
compresses seat count faster than it adds product — so the interesting moment is
when the market prices the threat before the numbers show it, or ignores it after
they do.

## Payment rails, and the people routing around them
**Looking for:** tollbooths on consumption with decades of pricing power, and the
challengers trying to disintermediate them. Held in tension deliberately: a
thesis on one is implicitly a thesis on the other.

## Space and defence as sovereign infrastructure
**Looking for:** capability that governments must buy domestically rather than
import, where the customer is price-insensitive and the contract is long. Mostly
pre-profit, so position size is the risk control, not the entry price.

## Electrification, and the grid that has to carry it
**Looking for:** vehicles, batteries and the transmission and power equipment
underneath. The vehicle makers are a price war; the equipment underneath is a
shortage. Prefer the shortage.

## Brands with pricing power independent of the cycle
**Looking for:** businesses that raise prices through a downturn without losing
volume. They are almost never cheap, which is exactly why the trigger is a
sector-wide de-rating rather than anything company-specific.

## Healthcare trading below its own history
**Looking for:** large-cap pharma and payers where a patent cliff, a pipeline
doubt, or a regulatory scare has compressed the multiple against that company's
own past. The test is whether the doubt is priced, not whether it is real.

## Underwriters of risk
**Looking for:** insurers, reinsurers and alternative asset managers — businesses
that earn on other people's capital and volatility. Bought for a hardening
pricing cycle, sold when discipline goes.

## Networks that already won
**Looking for:** marketplaces and platforms past the land-grab, where the network
effect is settled and the argument is about margin and capital return rather than
growth.

## Candidates

Names worth owning at the right price but not currently held. Grouped by the
theme each serves.

### The physical layer under AI

## AMD — €419.10
**Thesis:** the only credible merchant alternative to Nvidia in accelerators,
with server CPU share gains funding the attempt.
**Would need:** a named hyperscaler deploying MI-series at scale, at a multiple
below its own five-year median.

## Broadcom — €370.50
**Thesis:** custom accelerator design plus datacentre networking — levered to
hyperscaler capex but with switching costs the merchant silicon vendors lack.
**Would need:** a de-rating on a capex scare while the XPU backlog holds.

## Applied Materials — €470.15
**Thesis:** deposition and etch toolbase; every leading-edge transition goes
through it, and the cycle is the entry point rather than the risk.
**Would need:** trough wafer-fab-equipment guidance already in the price.

## Applied Optoelectronics — €120.68
**Thesis:** optical transceivers for datacentre interconnect — direct exposure to
the bandwidth constraint, at small scale and with heavy customer concentration.
**Would need:** a design win converting into multi-quarter revenue, not one order.

## Vertiv — €236.60
**Thesis:** power and thermal management for datacentres. The constraint is
physical and the backlog is contracted.
**Would need:** order growth intact through a multiple compression.

## Samsung (GDR) — €3,530.00
**Thesis:** memory and HBM with foundry optionality, permanently at a conglomerate
discount.
**Would need:** HBM qualification progress at a major accelerator customer.

## IREN — €36.17
**Thesis:** a bitcoin miner converting to AI datacentre hosting — owns the two
things that are actually scarce, power contracts and shells.
**Would need:** contracted AI hosting revenue genuinely replacing mining economics,
not running alongside it.

### Software sold by the seat

## SAP — €179.64
**Thesis:** ERP is the hardest software in the world to leave, and the cloud
migration is still only part-done.
**Would need:** cloud backlog growth holding while the multiple de-rates.

## Salesforce — €167.00
**Thesis:** the seat-count question in its sharpest form — the CRM incumbent with
the most to lose and the largest installed base to defend.
**Would need:** evidence agent revenue offsets seat pressure, in reported numbers.

## Workday — €154.24
**Thesis:** system of record for HR and finance; the stickiest possible software
sold on the most AI-exposed metric there is, headcount.
**Would need:** a pricing model that stops being per-employee.

## Figma — €20.10
**Thesis:** design collaboration with genuine network effects, still finding its
public multiple.
**Would need:** a post-lockup de-rating with net retention intact.

## Wix — €52.20
**Thesis:** website building moving upmarket, where AI site generation is both the
threat and the product.
**Would need:** bookings growth durable against free AI generators.

## Crowdstrike (A) — €185.22
**Thesis:** security platform consolidation — the budget line that survives cuts.
**Would need:** a de-rating. Quality this obvious rarely gets cheap.

## Palantir — €148.86
**Thesis:** deployment capability in government and commercial that competitors
have not replicated. Also serves the space and defence theme.
**Would need:** a very large de-rating. This is the one name here where the price,
not the business, is the entire question.

### Payment rails

## Visa — €313.60
**Thesis:** a tollbooth on global consumption with decades of demonstrated pricing
power.
**Would need:** a regulatory-scare de-rating below its own ten-year median.

## MasterCard — €485.70
**Thesis:** the same network economics at smaller share, so slightly more room to
grow into.
**Would need:** the same — a scare that compresses the multiple without changing
the volumes.

## PayPal — €51.02
**Thesis:** branded checkout still has real scale; the market prices it as if the
share loss is terminal.
**Would need:** branded checkout TPV stabilising for consecutive quarters.

## Klarna Group — €17.02
**Thesis:** BNPL at genuine scale, newly listed, with an untested credit book.
**Would need:** a full credit cycle of loss data before the thesis is even
assessable.

### Space and defence

## Rocket Lab — €73.70
**Thesis:** small launch cash-generating while Neutron and space systems are built
on top of it.
**Would need:** Neutron flying, and revenue mix shifting past launch.

## AST SpaceMobile — €62.70
**Thesis:** direct-to-handset satellite connectivity — binary on constellation
deployment, with carrier agreements already signed.
**Would need:** commercial service revenue, not further agreements.

## Axon Enterprise — €494.60
**Thesis:** law-enforcement hardware with recurring software attached, close to a
monopoly in its category.
**Would need:** a de-rating, which has almost never happened.

### Electrification

## BYD — €10.16
**Thesis:** vertically integrated from battery to vehicle, exporting its way out of
the domestic price war.
**Would need:** overseas margin holding while the China price war continues.

## Volkswagen (Vz.) — €76.14
**Thesis:** legacy auto mid-transition, on preference shares, at a discount that
already assumes the China business is impaired.
**Would need:** an EV margin inflection, or a cost programme with numbers attached.

### Brands with pricing power

## LVMH — €95.60
**Thesis:** the luxury conglomerate — brand portfolio wide enough to survive any
single label going cold.
**Would need:** a China demand trough visibly already in the price.

## Hermes — €1,630.50
**Thesis:** the least cyclical name in luxury, with supply deliberately constrained
below demand.
**Would need:** a sector-wide de-rating. Nothing company-specific will do it.

## Lululemon Athletica — €111.20
**Thesis:** premium athleisure where brand heat, not the economy, is the variable.
**Would need:** US comparable sales stabilising.

## Nintendo — €44.99
**Thesis:** an IP library that outlives every hardware cycle, monetised across
software, film and parks.
**Would need:** software attach rate after the current hardware launch settles.

### Healthcare below its own history

## Roche — €395.60
**Thesis:** pharma plus diagnostics, where pipeline scepticism has compressed the
multiple against its own past.
**Would need:** a phase-3 readout that changes the pipeline view.

## Novartis — €134.56
**Thesis:** a pure-play pharma post-Sandoz, with a clean balance sheet.
**Would need:** visibility on the patent cliff, not reassurance about it.

## Pfizer — €23.02
**Thesis:** post-COVID hangover, high yield, and an acquired pipeline the market
does not credit.
**Would need:** evidence the acquired pipeline actually replaces the cliff.

## UnitedHealth Group — €351.40
**Thesis:** scale in managed care that regulation compresses but does not remove.
**Would need:** the medical loss ratio stabilising.

## Ottobock — €57.90
**Thesis:** prosthetics and orthotics — structural demand growth, recently listed,
thinly covered.
**Would need:** a full year of public reporting before the numbers mean anything.

### Underwriters of risk

## Allianz — €435.20
**Thesis:** insurance plus asset management, where the asset management earnings
are valued as if they were insurance earnings.
**Would need:** a soft-cycle de-rating.

## Münchener Rück — €514.20
**Thesis:** reinsurance at or near the top of a hard pricing cycle, with a long
record of reserving conservatively.
**Would need:** pricing discipline holding as the cycle softens — the whole thesis
turns on that and nothing else.

## KKR & Co — €88.34
**Thesis:** fee-related earnings from permanent capital, plus balance-sheet
optionality the market discounts inconsistently.
**Would need:** fee-related earnings growth intact through a fundraising trough.

### Networks that already won

## Uber — €65.01
**Thesis:** mobility and delivery past the subsidy war and now cash generative;
autonomy is the swing factor in both directions.
**Would need:** a defensible view on whether AV is a threat or a supply channel.

## Scout24 — €74.55
**Thesis:** German property classifieds with a settled network effect and pricing
power over agents.
**Would need:** a de-rating driven by German housing weakness rather than by the
platform.

## Netflix — €64.09
**Thesis:** the streaming winner, now extending into advertising and live events at
much better incremental margin.
**Would need:** advertising ARPU disclosed well enough to model.

### Emerging markets at a governance discount

## Tencent Holdings — €52.73
**Thesis:** gaming, WeChat and a large investment portfolio, permanently discounted
for regulatory and governance risk.
**Would need:** a view on whether that discount narrows — if it never does, the
business quality is irrelevant.

## Never

Names and categories not to raise again, with the reason. Without this the same
idea resurfaces every few weeks and gets re-litigated each time.

The reason matters as much as the name: "declined, too cyclical for the horizon"
tells the agent what else to filter; a bare ticker does not.

- Anything pre-revenue, whatever the story
- Businesses whose earnings depend on a commodity price with no view attached
- Analyst upgrades, price-target changes and "unusual options activity" on any
  name in this file — the same exclusions as `watchlist.md`
